# cents.

A calm, private expense tracker that runs right in your browser. No app store, no account, no sign-up — just type what you spent and see exactly where your money went.

![cents. dashboard](screenshots/dashboard-desktop.png)

## In plain English

**cents.** keeps everything on your device. By default nothing is uploaded anywhere — only if you *choose* to turn on sync does a copy leave your browser. It helps you set a monthly budget, see what's safe to spend today, track the balances on your accounts and gift cards, and never miss a recurring bill.

## What you can do

- **Log expenses just by typing** — e.g. `lidl groceries 4.47 revolut`. The app works out the amount, category, and payment method for you.
- **Keep a budget** — set a monthly target and see your spend, what's left, and a "safe to spend today" number at a glance.
- **Track balances** — bank accounts (AIB, Revolut) and gift cards (Tesco, Lidl, Aldi), reconciled against your real statements.
- **Use your own payment methods** — **☰ → Payment methods** lists every method the entry form offers (the bank accounts, the gift cards, cash) beside the ones you add yourself (*Credit Union*, *Apple Pay*, whatever you actually pay with). Add a name and it appears in every picker at once — the entry form, transfers, split payments, recurring bills and the History filter. **Delete** takes one out of the pickers (a built-in says **Hide**, because the balance screen reconciles against it); either way the records you already saved keep the method they were recorded with and the row moves under *Removed from the pickers* with **Restore**, so tidying the list up can never cost you history.
- **Never miss a bill** — rent, subscriptions, and paydays as recurring reminders, with one-tap marking done.
- **Undo anything** — every action can be undone, even after a reload.
- **Light or dark** — the app opens **dark** by default; **☰ → Appearance** (or the toggle in the header) switches it, and your choice is remembered on the device.
- **Your data is yours to take out** — **☰ → Data** shows the entry count, the document's own size on this device (and whether the browser has promised to keep it: *persistent* or *best-effort*), and — when sync is on — whether the **GitHub copy of your data matches the cloud copy**, which is re-checked every time that menu is opened. **Export data** writes the JSON the app restores from; **Export CSV** writes the same ledger as one row per entry (date, type, category, details, amount, method, note) with the labels you see on screen, for a spreadsheet; **Restore from backup** reads a `.json` export back and *merges* it — newest version of each entry wins, and nothing on the device is deleted, so restoring an old copy cannot lose what you have; a document the browser could not parse is kept rather than dropped, and **Recover unreadable copy** appears in that menu to hand it back (as a merge when it parses, as a download of the raw bytes when it does not — those bytes are the only copy of that ledger).
- **Made to be installed on a phone** — add it to your home screen and it opens with no browser chrome, like an app: Android's **back gesture closes whatever sheet is open** (and never the lock screen), there is no pull-to-refresh to reload it mid-scroll, and long-pressing the launcher icon offers **Add an entry**, **History**, **Tesco** and **Insights** directly. Sharing a line *into* cents. from another app — a payment notification, a bank message — drops it straight into the quick entry box, previewed but not saved, so you check it before it becomes an entry.
- **Stay private** — the app opens locked (default PIN `100901`); on your phone you can unlock with fingerprint or Face ID instead.
- **Tesco Pay tab** — log your shifts with the form, book a whole week off with **Week off** (any day of that week and the hours of pay: the row lands on the week's Sunday and pays at your normal rate, not the Sunday premium), or write them the way you would say them: **Quick entry** takes one shift per line (`mon 8-4 stock take`, `yesterday 12:30-20:15`, `fri 20:00-01:00`, `week off 7h`, `13/09 9-5 special`) and prices every line against your own rate card in a live preview before anything is stored — a line it cannot read is named back on its own, in red, and the batch is held until every line reads (a half-added batch is how a shift goes missing from a week), while lines that are already stored are dropped rather than duplicated and the toast says how many actually landed. Or point the camera at the roster and let it read them (**Use camera** on the Scan roster card, beside *Choose roster photo*: one shot, or a **live read** that keeps taking frames until your shifts are found). The reader levels a tilted sheet before parsing (up to 8.5° off square), turns a sideways photo upright, ignores the duplicated words a strip-based read produces, and takes the sheet's own printed rules as the column boundaries where they exist. It reads the sheet's own dialect: a colon that came back from the engine as a full stop is still a shift, a date may name its month and print its year, and the date range printed under the title is read even when the engine boxes the whole line as one word — and when that range is a day out of step with the day names (a Monday-first range over a Sunday-first sheet), the day names win, because a week dated a day late is the one misreading nobody catches. It does not stop at the first reading that mentions your name: every reading of the photo is scored on what it actually produced — the week's columns, your row, dated shifts on it, and how many of the sheet's own day names agree with the dates they were given — and the reader escalates until one comes back whole, evening out a photo lit from one side with a local threshold (a lamp at one end of a table, a phone's own shadow across the bottom third) before it gives up on it. The readings of a rung are taken together on a small pool of engines rather than one after another — the three strips, then the whole page and the thresholded page, then both quarter turns, and finally your name and the whole week of cells — so a scan waits on the slowest reading of a rung instead of on all of them in turn, and the answer is still the one the ladder would have reached on its own: every reading is scored on what it produced, and the rungs the ladder would not have got to are not believed. Each reading's image is prepared on a worker of its own — thresholded and encoded as a PNG away from the app's thread, which used to spend up to two thirds of a second of every strip unable to start the next read, collect the last one or move the progress bar. The scan card stays alive while the engine works: the bar steps, **Cancel** answers, and preparing a read is no longer the part of a scan that could not overlap it. That is what a photo taken at an angle off the noticeboard now takes, and a photo it cannot read with confidence is still refused rather than guessed at. The photo is decoded to a canvas, read and dropped — nothing is written to storage, cache or state — and the app works out paid hours, Sunday pay, weekly gross, an Irish PAYE/USC/PRSI/pension estimate, and tracks the tax on each payslip. The estimate follows the rules that surprise people at low hours: on €352 a week or less you pay **no PRSI at all** (not the full rate with a credit), and above that the €12 PRSI credit tapers away by €424 — so a €300 week of about 20 hours costs €2.54 in tax, the USC alone, with the weekly credit swallowing the PAYE. The rate itself is on the **Pay rules & tax rates** card (4.2% until 30 Sep 2026, 4.35% from 1 Oct 2026), where you can set yours. The home screen shows this month's hours, the net pay those hours should produce (**Anticipated (est. net)** — simulated after tax, the same figure each week card prints after its own estimate) and what the bank has actually paid in (**Received this month**) at a glance; both money figures are nets, so they compare directly, and *received* counts each week on its **pay date** (the week's Sunday + 11), which is why a week you have worked but not been paid for yet is not in it — while a worked week with no payslip typed in yet is worth its estimate rather than counted as nothing. **Export CSV** writes the whole table out in the workbook's own columns — the shifts, then the tax table below them — and **Import CSV** reads one back (dates, times, the special flag, notes, payslip tax and week numbers; hours, pay and the estimate are recalculated). Each week's number is the sheet's own count and can be edited on the week card; every payslip row prints that same number as a *Wk* chip, plus its **pay date** — the week's Sunday + 11 days, which is always the Thursday the money lands — beside the *Tax paid* figure that row records. Any shift row can be **edited**: the pencil beside it opens the row as a sheet (date, start, end, stated hours, an optional pay override, note, special rate) with a live preview of the row it will write, and the same rules the CSV import applies — so a shift can never be edited into something an import would have refused. Moving the date re-slots it into its new week, one undo step covers the change, and the row keeps its identity, so a synced copy elsewhere is updated rather than duplicated. When the money actually lands, type it into the **Bank** box on the week's first shift row: it is the week's *final bank pay*, one figure per week, and it feeds the week's *Received* line, the month's received total and the lifetime figure — leave it blank until you have been paid, and blank it again to un-say it (blank means *not known*, never €0). Until a week has a figure of its own it is estimated, and either way it is counted into the month it was **paid** in, not the month it was worked in. A week already carrying that figure on its payslip's **Bank** field keeps the two in step, so there is one figure rather than two that disagree. Whatever the tab records for a week — the figure from your payslip, or the **Bank** box — also lands in your ledger on that week's pay date, as the *Tesco pay* entry the payday tracker used to ask you to fill in by hand: it appears on its own, it follows a corrected figure, a line you have edited or deleted is left exactly as you left it, and undoing a recording takes the entry back out with it.
## Getting started

1. **Open the app** — run it locally (below) or open your deployed version.
2. **Unlock** — the default PIN is `100901`. Change it in **☰ → Privacy & security**.
3. **Set a budget** — the monthly budget box is right on the dashboard.
4. **Log something** — hit **Add** and type something like `coffee 3.50`.

## Privacy & optional sync

- **By default, everything stays on your device.** Your data lives in your browser's local storage; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Optional cloud sync** — want the same data on your phone and laptop? Sync holds the account's document in the deployment's own **KV store** (on Cloudflare, where the site runs) and mirrors it into a **private GitHub repo that only you control** — a readable backup, and the way back if the site's stored data is ever lost. Your login token never touches the browser. Set it up in **☰ → Cloud sync**.

### Profiles — sharing cents. with friends

cents. is built to be shared: you keep one deployed site, and everyone who uses it gets their **own account inside it** — their own entries, balances, budget and PIN — signed in on **their own phone**, in their own browser, with their own password. Nobody sees anyone else's spending, and switching accounts never mixes them up.

**☰ → Switch profile** (or the chip in the header, or the “Who's tracking?” screen) moves between accounts. A switch happens in place — the page does not reload — and it always lands on a prompt: pick the person, then that person's PIN, so nobody is ever asked for a PIN before an identity. The account you were using keeps its own copy in the browser's storage, untouched.

Every profile also gets a **picture**: pick one from **☰ → Profile picture** (or **Change picture** on the “Who's tracking?” screen) — eighteen icons, or keep the initial. It is drawn as an inline icon on a colour derived from the profile, so there are no image files to download and the colour stays put when the picture changes. Like the name, the picture lives on the device and does not sync — the admin can set it for anyone from the Admin panel.

Each account keeps its own document in the cloud — private, in the deployment's KV store, mirrored to `data-<name>.json` when a mirror is configured — and needs **its own secret** (`Sam` → `APP_PASSWORD_SAM`); an account with no variable still works, but locally only — it is never handed the default password, which is what keeps accounts from reading each other. The Admin panel names the exact variable to create.

A **setup link** (**☰ → Cloud sync → Create link**) carries the account with it: send it to your friend and, opened on their phone, it offers to add that account with the same name and password instead of asking them to retype either. That is how someone gets their own copy on their own device.

A profile's own space is device-local and never syncs itself. Deleting one removes that account's data on that device — a copy already in the cloud is left alone.

### Admin panel — you, the owner

The **owner** account (`Personal`, the one whose password is `APP_PASSWORD`) is the admin: it is the only account that can create, rename or delete the others. Two ways in, both after you unlock: the **Admin** pill in the header, and **☰ → Admin** — the first row of the menu, tinted so it stands out (`People & tab access`). Everyone else's menu says *Switch profile* there instead, and has no header button. Everyone else sees the picker, which for them is only a way to switch. The owner account cannot be deleted — it holds the site password and the base cloud file, so removing it would leave nobody able to open the panel.

**Invite** is how someone gets their account onto their own phone. Tapping it mints a **single-use, 30-day link** for that account — it carries their own password (which you never have to know or type: it lives in Netlify as `APP_PASSWORD_<NAME>`) and the tabs you currently allow them, so the account arrives already configured. Send it to them directly: while it is unspent, whoever holds it can read and write that account's data. Opening it on their phone adds the account, switches to it and starts syncing their own copy — your device keeps only the account itself, never their entries.

Each person's card also has a **Picture** button, so you can give everyone a face without passing a phone around.

Under each person the panel shows a row of **tab chips**: switch one off and they no longer see the tab — not in the nav, not in the menu, and not by typing the URL (a hidden route lands back on the dashboard, so a bookmark is not a way around it). The entry points on the dashboard go with it: hide *Add* and **New entry** disappears, hide *Balances* and the top-up and **Manage** links do, hide *Tesco* and the pay widget and payday tracker do.

Two things worth knowing:

- **A hidden tab is a UI policy, not a lock.** Anyone holding a phone reads everything on it. Hiding a tab keeps the app tidy for the person using it; it does not keep a determined one out of anything.
- **The dashboard always stays visible.** It is the landing page and the only screen that reaches the others, so it is deliberately not in the toggles.

The setting is stored in *that person's own document*, which means it travels to every device they sign in on the next time that account syncs — so a restriction set here sticks after they switch phones. Switching a tab off also writes it straight into that account's cloud file (through `profiles-admin`, authorised by your own password), so it lands without waiting for them to open the app; only that one setting is written, never their entries. If the account has not synced yet there is no document to carry it, and the app says so — send a fresh invite, which carries the tabs with it.

## Self-hosting notes (Cloudflare Pages)

The whole site is one host: the static app, the functions that answer the browser's calls under `/api/*` (the same three also sit under the pre-rename `/.netlify/functions/*` spelling, which is what a device set up before the rename still has saved, and which the app rewrites to `/api/*` the next time it saves its settings), and a KV namespace — all served by the **Cloudflare Pages** project `cents-app`. Pages compiles `functions/` (the catch-all `functions/[[path]].js`, which hands every request to the router in `functions/_router.js`) and the build stages the repository through `.assetsignore`, so the upload is exactly what this repo says is published. A push to `main` publishes it; nothing is deployed from a shell, and local testing is `npx wrangler pages dev . --kv CENTS_KV` — same config, same function compilation, and the store bound under the name the code reads (`pages dev` takes the KV namespace from that flag rather than from the config, which is why it is spelled out). Non-secret coordinates live in `wrangler.toml`'s `[vars]`; everything below is a secret and is set on the Pages project (Settings → Variables and Secrets), or in `.dev.vars` for a local run.

The optional sync feature needs:

| Variable | What it's for |
|---|---|
| `CENTS_SECRET` | Recommended — the deployment's **root secret**: the single-use recovery links are encrypted with it. Any 16+ character random string you keep to yourself. Set it once and keep it: changing it invalidates every outstanding recovery link. |
| `APP_PASSWORD` | The password you type under **☰ → Cloud sync** (at least 8 characters). It gates sync, for the default **Personal** profile. |
| `APP_PASSWORD_<NAME>` | Optional — one per person you share with (**☰ → Admin → Add profile**). `Sam` becomes `APP_PASSWORD_SAM`; the app names the exact variable in the Admin panel. An account with no variable stays local: it is never granted the default password, so the accounts stay isolated from each other. |
| `GITHUB_TOKEN` | **Optional** — a fine-grained GitHub token with **contents: read and write**, for the data repo only. It never leaves the function. Without it there is no GitHub mirror (every document still lives in KV) and no way to read a copy an earlier deployment left in the repo. |
| `REPO_OWNER` | **Optional** — owner of the data repo (your GitHub username or org). One of the mirror's coordinates. |
| `REPO_NAME` | **Optional** — the **private** repo each account's copy is mirrored into. |
| `REPO_BRANCH` | Optional — branch the data file lives on. Default `main`. |
| `REPO_FILE` | Optional — path of the mirrored data file in that repo. Default `data.json`. |

Secrets are set on the Pages project, not in a file: the app passwords, `CENTS_SECRET` and `GITHUB_TOKEN` live under Settings → Variables and Secrets, and nowhere in this repository.

A deployment with no `GITHUB_TOKEN`, or without the repo coordinates, has **no mirror** rather than a broken one: every document still lives in KV, the Data menu says the mirror is *Off*, and no read or save fails for want of it. A local run is always that deployment — `wrangler pages dev` reads `.dev.vars` but does **not** apply `wrangler.toml`'s `[vars]`, so its repo coordinates are absent unless they are passed on the command line (`--binding REPO_OWNER=…`).

The guard suite needs **Node 22 or newer** (CI pins it): the guard scripts import the app's browser modules as ES modules, and older Node parses them as CommonJS and dies.

Each account's synced document lives in the deployment's KV namespace (CENTS_KV), with the GitHub file written as a mirror on every save *when a token is set* — **after** the save has been committed, never before it: a save waits on KV and on nothing else, and the mirror write is handed to the request's `waitUntil`, so a slow or failing code host can neither delay a save nor hold a version the store refused. The first read of an account that is not there yet is served *from* the mirror and then seeds KV, so an existing deployment upgrades without losing anything; after that the mirror is only ever written. With no `GITHUB_TOKEN` the store is KV only — the same "one durable primary" shape the site ran on before — and no read or save fails for want of a mirror.

**Because that mirror can fail without the save noticing, the two copies are compared after every save.** The function reads the mirror file back and hashes it against the version KV holds; the result is recorded with the document and shown in **☰ → Data** as one line — *GitHub mirror: In sync*, or *Behind* / *Different copy* / *Not in the repo* / *Not checked*, in the warning colour, with the sentence that says what happened. The line re-checks the moment that menu is opened, and a gap also raises a one-off notice at the time it appears or clears. A comparison nobody has carried out yet reads as *pending* rather than as a pass, and a deployment with no token reads as *Off* — neither is a failure, and neither is reported as good news either.

## Run it locally

Browsers block the file when opened by double-click, so run a tiny local server instead. From this folder:

```
./serve.sh
```

or any simple static server, e.g. `python -m http.server 8080` — then open the address it prints.

### Getting your Tesco Pay data in

**Tesco Pay → Import** reads the workbook itself: drop `Tesco Pay.xlsx` (or `.xlsm`) straight in and the app reads the **Data** and **Summary** sheets together — no export step, no converter, nothing installed. A CSV of the workbook works too. Dates are normalised whatever the file held (Excel serials, `13/09/2026`, `09/13/2026`, `13 Sep 2026`); an ambiguous one can be fixed on the mapping screen before anything is written.

## Look and feel

Hand-written CSS, no framework, one emerald accent. The palette, the radii and the easing curve are tokens at the top of `styles.css` alongside the dark theme, and almost nothing moves: the surfaces are flat, the borders are hairlines, and the only motion left is a route change's own settle, a morphing icon on the phone tab bar, a headline figure counting to its new value, and a fade on the things that appear *into* a screen you are already reading (the bulk-action bar, an empty state). A donut that drew itself in on every repaint, a confetti burst at the month's end, a Save button that bounced, a spotlit card that followed the cursor and a pair of tinted bands at the phone's edges were all removed — each was a delight rather than information, and each cost a repaint on a screen someone was trying to read.

**Switching tabs is instant, and that is a measured property rather than a preference.** A route change renders the next screen and stops there: no screen entrance, no staggered cards, no view transition. It used to hand the change to the View Transitions API, which cost far more than its animation — a browser will not run the update callback until it has captured the old state, and capturing needs a compositor frame, so a document that cannot produce one waited out the whole 250ms fallback budget before drawing anything. On a non-compositing webview that was **~270ms per tab switch, ~250 of it the budget, against ~36ms** for the same navigation with no transition — before counting the 180/220ms cross-fade on the engines where it did work. `scripts/test-motion.mjs` pins the machinery's absence, so it cannot come back quietly.

**The same rule was then applied to the menu, which nobody had measured.** Every row used to spring in on its own — eight staggered delays ramping to 0.125s — on top of the panel's slide, so opening the menu *and* every drill into a sub-level ran **19–21 live animations and took 285ms to come to rest**, for rows you had just asked to see. The rows now arrive with the panel, and the only motion left is the level's directional slide and the trigger's burger-to-X morph, which is the tap's own feedback. It settles in **160ms with 6 animations**. Every duration in that section is held under a 0.2s ceiling by the guard, so the latency cannot creep back one rule at a time.

The animated backgrounds went the same way. The phone edge bands carried the last `backdrop-filter` in the stylesheet; a fixed, masked strip that re-samples the page behind it does that work on **every scrolled frame, for the whole length of the page**. The header, the modal scrims and the dark toggle had each already given theirs up for exactly this reason, so the bands now dissolve the edge with a tint and a mask — the same treatment the file described as its no-support fallback. There are no `backdrop-filter` declarations left anywhere, and that is pinned too.

Charts can be **read**, not just looked at. Hover (or tap) a month on the *Month by month* bars for that month's spending and income together, a slice of the category donut for its share and its money, or one of the spend chart's coloured days for the date and the total. The month's whole column is the target, not the 10px bar.

Headline figures **count when they change**: the month total, the running balance and the net-worth figure on the home screen tween from the value you last saw to the new one, and the last frame writes the formatted result — a total can never come to rest on an animation frame. A figure that has not changed does not move at all (every keystroke repaints the screen), and one you have never seen simply arrives rather than counting up from zero. You can see it by adding an entry and going back to the home screen.

`prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured twice over: in CSS every animation and transition is switched off, and in JS the two things that are not CSS — the donut draw-in and the counting figures — land on their final frame immediately.

## Checks

Twenty-three pure-Node guardrails run in CI, and they are what you run before a deploy; `run-guards.mjs` runs all twenty-three in order and **stops at the first failure**. That single command is also what CI calls:

```
node scripts/run-guards.mjs            # all twenty-three, in order
node scripts/check-worker-config.mjs   # wrangler.toml + functions/_router.js: the Pages project, the route table the browser calls, the KV binding, that the removed daily reminder has stayed removed, that no secret sits in a committed file, the CI wiring, and the docs below
node scripts/check-sw-shell.mjs        # every offline-shell entry exists on disk
node scripts/test-image-hygiene.mjs    # the image guard catches what it was written to catch: planted fixtures for every forbidden call, every tombstone, the walk's depth and its skip list
node scripts/check-image-hygiene.mjs   # no photo can enter the app: the roster-photo pipeline stays deleted, and no shipped file can capture or hold an image
node scripts/check-csp-hashes.mjs      # every inline boot script is hashed in the CSP
node scripts/check-freshness.mjs       # a deploy reaches the device: every asset revalidates, the worker is network-first, the build baseline a redeploy is detected against lives in Cache Storage rather than in the worker's own memory, and no .assetsignore rule can remove a file the app loads
node scripts/check-icon-refs.mjs       # every icon lookup resolves (a miss renders an empty element, silently)
node scripts/check-boot-payload.mjs    # what the app parses before first paint stays the shell + the pay arithmetic; every lazy import() resolves
node scripts/check-app-shell.mjs       # an installed copy behaves like an app: the manifest identifies it, every icon and screenshot is really there at the size it claims, a launcher shortcut and the share target point at routes the router still has, Android's back gesture closes the top sheet without ever dismissing the lock, and the page cannot be pull-to-refreshed
node scripts/check-a11y.mjs            # the a11y harness still covers every route, menu level and menu modal; no icon-only nav control is unnamed
node scripts/check-mobile-layout.mjs   # a stat value reaches its card formatted and sized for its own length; the card and the value cannot spill it; six dock tabs compress instead of hanging off the screen
node scripts/check-css-tokens.mjs      # the stylesheet is a closed system: every custom property read is defined, every @keyframes is animated by something, and the braces balance

node scripts/test-integrity.mjs        # storage/merge cannot lose, corrupt or resurrect data (including the payment methods you own, the one date rule, and the document's own shape)
node scripts/test-parity-money.mjs     # the money rules a typed line goes through: the quick-entry parser (lifted from app.js and executed, not copied), cents-only precision (a third decimal is refused, never truncated or rounded), and the split/transfer groups that must survive a save/load round trip
node scripts/test-load-existing.mjs    # a document the shipping app wrote loads in the ported core and round-trips unchanged (the frozen fixture is built by scripts/make-cents-fixture.mjs)
node scripts/check-pin-screens.mjs     # the browser leg below still loads that fixture, in a fresh context, behind the test PIN under the production CSP — and stays a hand-run leg rather than a CI dependency
node scripts/test-profiles.mjs         # profiles stay isolated, and their cloud credentials never fall back
node scripts/test-tesco.mjs            # the pay/tax engine still matches the workbook's figures
node scripts/test-sync.mjs             # the sync contract still round-trips all state
node scripts/test-cloud.mjs            # the KV store is the primary, GitHub the mirror, and the version token decides conflicts
node scripts/test-failure-throttle.mjs # the failed-auth damper reads the client-IP header THIS host sets (cf-connecting-ip first, Netlify's second, never x-forwarded-for), and a caller that sends its own cannot choose its bucket
node scripts/test-motion.mjs           # a headline figure counts only when it changed and rests on the exact total; a route change renders and waits on nothing (no transition, no entrance animation)
node scripts/test-ui-libs.mjs          # the vendored UI libraries do what the app assumes: the search ranks through the vendored Fuse, the focus trap's load order and Escape rules hold, every vendor path resolves
```

There is no longer a canvas harness to read, because there is no longer any canvas: a roster photo cannot enter the app at all. `97fb40f` removed the reader, its engine pool, the prep workers and the camera modal, and `check-image-hygiene.mjs` pins that absence — the removed files as tombstones, and a scan of every shipped file for a capture or pixel call, so the pipeline cannot come back as a module beside the old one. What stayed is the part that was always Node-testable: the text grammar (`parseRosterWords`, `columnAnchors`, `resolveColumnDates`, `rosterColumnBounds`), which takes plain word lists and is pinned in `scripts/test-tesco.mjs`.

Gate C's browser half has the same constraint and a different answer, because it is an assertion rather than an audit a person reads. `scripts/pin-screens.browser.mjs` is a **Playwright** leg: it starts a loopback server over this checkout under the production CSP, opens a fresh browser context (empty storage, asserted), writes the frozen document under `cents:p/personal/state`, unlocks the rendered app with the test PIN `100901`, and checks every screen against the document — net worth, budget, income and net, the three Hairline read-outs, the eight ledger rows (with the split pair grouped and the tombstone gone), the balance checkpoint and per-account movements, the insights totals, the Tesco shift, and the custom category and method — then confirms it wrote nothing but the default profile and left the document deep-equal. The clock is pinned a day after the fixture's newest entry, because the fixture is frozen in one month and the dashboard is keyed on "this month".

```
node scripts/pin-screens.browser.mjs    # by hand, before the cutover
```

Playwright is not a dependency of this repo, and adding it would put a browser download in front of every deploy, so the leg is deliberately outside `run-guards.mjs` and CI. It resolves Playwright from `$CENTS_PLAYWRIGHT`, this repo, or the shared tooling project's install (`../design-system`) in this workspace, and it **fails** rather than skips when it cannot find it. `check-pin-screens.mjs` is what keeps a hand-run leg honest: it pins the fixture, the real key, the test PIN, the fresh context, the frozen clock, the production CSP, the loopback-only origin and the synthetic-ledger rule, and it fails if the leg is dropped from README/CLAUDE.md or quietly moved into the dependency-free suite.

Accessibility is audited the same way, because a Node script cannot read a rendered DOM: open `a11y-harness.html` on the served app and it drives the real thing in an iframe through every route and every surface that *opens* (the menu and its drill-downs, and the modals they reach — never a click inside an open modal, so the ledger it audits is untouched), reporting per surface the four defects a rebuild quietly reintroduces: a control with no accessible name, two controls in one scope sharing an `aria-label` (an addressed identity here, not a label), a form field with no label, and an `<img>` with no `alt`. Keep its tab in the foreground: the app defers its render to an animation frame and a background tab is throttled, so a hidden harness audits nothing. `check-a11y.mjs` is what stops the harness from quietly ceasing to cover the app: it asserts the surfaces against `seed.js`'s tabs, against every menu level the app can drill into and against every modal the menu opens, and it fails if the harness's verdict stops counting any of the four defects.

`.github/workflows/guards.yml` runs the same suite on every push, under the Node version the suite needs. That matters for one specific reason: the deploy reads `wrangler.toml` before any of this code runs, so a broken config can never be reported by a check that runs later — the only signal is a failed deploy. CI is the earliest place it can be caught, and `check-worker-config.mjs` catches most of that class statically, with no wrangler install needed.

If you edit one of the inline scripts in `index.html`, re-hash it in the CSP in `_headers`: `check-csp-hashes` prints the exact hash to paste, and the browser would otherwise refuse to run that script in production only (no CSP is sent locally).

## Built with

Hand-written **HTML / CSS / JavaScript** (no framework), a **service worker** so the app works offline after you first visit, and a single **Cloudflare Worker** for hosting — the static app and its functions together, with a **KV** namespace holding the optional cloud copies.
